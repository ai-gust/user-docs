Triggers let aiGust watch your clients' financial data continuously on your behalf. Instead of manually reviewing every client every month to spot anomalies, you define the conditions that matter — a cash balance falling below a safe level, margins compressing beyond a tolerance, or a creditor days ratio spiking — and aiGust notifies you the moment those conditions are met. This keeps you ahead of problems and gives you the opportunity to reach out to clients proactively, before issues escalate.

## What triggers are

A trigger is a rule attached to one or more client administrations. It defines a metric, a condition, and a threshold. When aiGust syncs financial data and evaluates the rule, a passing condition generates an **alert** that appears in your dashboard and alerts inbox.

Triggers are evaluated each time new data is synced from the connected accounting system. For most integrations this occurs daily, so alerts typically arrive within 24 hours of the underlying transaction data changing.

## Types of triggers

aiGust supports three categories of trigger rules:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="KPI thresholds" icon="gauge-high" href="/concepts/kpis">
    Fire when a KPI crosses an absolute value — for example, when gross profit margin falls below 30% or when the current ratio drops below 1.0.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Period-over-period changes" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left" href="/concepts/financial-periods">
    Fire when a metric changes by more than a specified percentage between two periods — for example, when revenue drops more than 15% month-on-month.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ratio limits" icon="percent" href="/reports/other-ratios">
    Fire when a calculated ratio exceeds a defined band — for example, when staff costs as a percentage of revenue exceed 45%.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  You can combine multiple conditions within a single trigger using **AND** logic. For example, you can create a trigger that only fires when both revenue is down more than 10% AND the cash balance is below £10,000. This reduces noise from triggers that would otherwise fire in isolation.
</Note>

## Creating a trigger for an administration

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the administration">
    Navigate to the relevant client and select the administration you want to monitor. Open the **Insights** tab and click **Triggers & Alerts** in the left navigation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click 'Add trigger'">
    Select **Add trigger** in the top-right corner of the Triggers & Alerts screen. The trigger builder panel opens on the right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the trigger type">
    Select one of the three trigger categories: **KPI threshold**, **Period-over-period change**, or **Ratio limit**. The available metrics update to reflect your selection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the metric and set the threshold">
    Choose the specific metric from the dropdown. Enter the threshold value and select the condition direction: **above**, **below**, or **changes by more than**. For period-over-period triggers, also select the comparison period (month-on-month, quarter-on-quarter, or year-on-year).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure alert delivery">
    Choose how you want to be notified. Options include the **aiGust alerts inbox** (always on), **email notification**, and **in-app banner**. You can also assign the alert to a specific team member if you manage clients across a team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name and save the trigger">
    Give the trigger a descriptive name (for example, "Gross margin below 30%") so it is easy to identify in the alerts inbox. Click **Save trigger** to activate it immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You can apply the same trigger to multiple administrations at once using the **Apply to multiple clients** option on the final save step. This is useful for practice-wide thresholds, such as monitoring cash ratios across all clients in a particular sector.
</Tip>

## Viewing active alerts

When a trigger fires, the resulting alert appears in two places:

**Dashboard alert panel.** The main aiGust dashboard includes an **Active Alerts** panel that lists all unresolved alerts across your entire client portfolio. Each entry shows the client name, trigger name, current metric value, and the date the alert fired. Click any row to jump directly to the affected administration.

**Alerts inbox.** The dedicated **Alerts** page (accessible from the left sidebar) provides a filterable, sortable list of all alerts — active, dismissed, and resolved. Use the filters to narrow by client, trigger type, severity, or date range.

<Note>
  Alerts are colour-coded by severity. Red alerts indicate conditions that typically require urgent attention (for example, a current ratio below 0.5). Amber alerts flag conditions that warrant monitoring. Severity is assigned automatically based on the distance between the current metric value and the trigger threshold.
</Note>

## Resolving or dismissing an alert

Each alert has two disposition options:

<Accordion title="Resolve an alert">
  Select **Resolve** when you have taken action in response to the alert and the situation is addressed. Resolved alerts are moved to the resolved archive and are no longer shown in the active panel. If the underlying metric crosses the threshold again in a future sync, a new alert is generated.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Dismiss an alert">
  Select **Dismiss** when the alert fired but no action is required — for example, the movement was planned and expected. Dismissed alerts are logged with a timestamp and optional note. Like resolved alerts, they are removed from the active view but remain auditable in the alerts inbox.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
  Dismissing an alert does not disable the trigger. If the metric crosses the threshold again in the next sync cycle, a new alert will be generated. If you no longer want to monitor a particular condition, disable or delete the trigger from the Triggers & Alerts settings screen.
</Warning>

## Using alerts to proactively contact clients

Alerts are most valuable when they prompt outreach before a client is even aware of a developing issue. Here is a practical workflow for turning alerts into advisory value:

**Triage alerts at the start of each day.** Review the Active Alerts panel each morning as part of your daily routine. This takes only a few minutes and ensures no critical signals are missed.

**Add a note before calling.** Before contacting a client, open the alert and add a short note summarising what you observed and what you plan to discuss. This keeps a record of your advisory activity tied directly to the data that prompted it.

**Reference the metric in your message.** When reaching out, cite the specific figure that triggered the alert. For example: "I noticed your debtor days have increased to 58 days this month, up from 41 days in the previous period — I wanted to check in and see if there is anything we can help with." This demonstrates that you are monitoring their business actively and builds trust.

**Follow up with a benchmarking report.** If an alert points to a metric that is also benchmarked, use the Benchmarking tab to show the client how their figure compares to sector peers. This adds context and reinforces the advisory recommendation.

<Tip>
  Create a set of standard practice-wide triggers for common risk indicators — such as a current ratio below 1.0, revenue down more than 20% year-on-year, or cash balance below one month's average expenditure — and apply them to your entire client portfolio as a baseline monitoring layer.
</Tip>
