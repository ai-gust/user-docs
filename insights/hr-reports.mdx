Staff costs are typically the largest single line item in a service business's profit and loss, yet they are often reviewed no more than once a year. aiGust's HR reports give you a continuous, structured view of your clients' people costs — headcount trends, cost-per-employee movements, and how labour costs compare to sector peers — so you can surface workforce-related insights in every client meeting, not just at year-end.

## What HR data aiGust pulls from connected accounting systems

aiGust derives HR data from the payroll and staff cost accounts within the connected accounting system. No separate HR platform integration is required. The data aiGust extracts includes:

- **Total payroll cost** — gross wages plus employer's National Insurance contributions and employer pension contributions, pulled from the relevant nominal accounts.
- **Headcount** — where payroll journal entries include employee-level detail, aiGust counts distinct employee references to derive headcount. Where only aggregate payroll entries are present, aiGust uses the total payroll cost divided by the average cost-per-employee for the sector to estimate headcount.
- **Payroll frequency** — monthly, four-weekly, or weekly, detected from the pattern of payroll journal entries.
- **Staff cost categories** — where the chart of accounts distinguishes between different employee groups (for example, directors' remuneration, administrative staff, production staff), aiGust reflects this breakdown in the reports.

<Note>
  The richness of HR report data depends on the level of detail in the connected accounting system. Clients who use a dedicated payroll system integrated with their accounting software, such as Xero Payroll or QuickBooks Payroll, will have more granular data available than those who import payroll as a single monthly journal.
</Note>

Navigate to **Insights → HR Reports** within any client administration to access the following reports:

## Reading staff cost trends

The staff costs report is the most frequently used HR report in aiGust. Here is how to interpret the key elements:

**Staff cost as a percentage of revenue.** This ratio is shown as a line overlay on the staff cost bar chart. A rising ratio indicates that staff costs are growing faster than revenue — often the first warning sign of margin pressure in a service business. A falling ratio suggests the business is becoming more efficient per pound of revenue, or that revenue growth is outpacing headcount growth.

**Employer on-costs breakdown.** The report separates gross wages from employer NI and employer pension. If you see pension costs rising disproportionately, this may indicate a move to a higher contribution tier under auto-enrolment rules — worth raising with the client if they are unaware of the change.

**Headcount-adjusted cost view.** Toggle **Cost per employee** to see whether a rising total staff cost is driven by headcount growth or by increased cost per person. These have very different advisory implications: headcount growth may be planned and positive, while unexpected per-employee cost increases may signal pay drift, regrading, or bonus payments that need reviewing.

<Tip>
  Use the **Year-on-year comparison** toggle to overlay the current period's staff cost trend against the same period in the prior year. This quickly reveals whether seasonal patterns in staffing are consistent or whether something unusual has occurred.
</Tip>

## Benchmarking staff costs against sector peers

aiGust integrates HR data directly into the benchmarking engine, so you can compare your client's staff costs against peers using the same sector, company type, and size-band matching as the main benchmarking report.

The following HR-related KPIs are included in the benchmarking peer comparison:

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Staff cost as % of revenue | Labour cost efficiency relative to turnover |
| Revenue per employee | Output productivity per head |
| Cost per employee | Average all-in employment cost per person |
| Headcount growth rate | Year-on-year change in employee numbers |

To view HR benchmarks, open **Insights → Benchmarking** and scroll to the **People & efficiency** section of the report. Each KPI card shows the client's current value, the peer median, and the client's quartile position.

<Accordion title="What a high staff cost ratio tells you">
  A staff cost ratio in the third or bottom quartile (above median, meaning costs are high relative to peers) may indicate over-staffing, above-market pay rates, excessive overtime, or a less efficient operating model than competitors. Use the HR reports to determine whether the ratio has been rising recently or has been consistently elevated, and explore with the client whether the premium labour cost is reflected in service quality, pricing, or other competitive advantages.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What a low revenue per employee tells you">
  Below-median revenue per employee suggests the business generates less output per head than comparable firms. This could reflect lower pricing, lower utilisation, higher support staff ratios, or an earlier stage of growth where capacity has been built ahead of demand. Understanding which of these explanations applies shapes the advisory response significantly.
</Accordion>

## Using HR insights during client meetings

HR data is often under-used in accounting advisory conversations, but it connects directly to the topics clients care most about — people, productivity, and cost control. Here are practical ways to use aiGust's HR reports in meetings:

**Bring the staff cost ratio to every meeting.** For service businesses, the staff cost ratio is as important as gross margin. Including it as a standard item in your regular reporting deck signals to clients that you are monitoring one of their most significant cost drivers continuously.

**Use cost-per-employee to frame pay review conversations.** When a client is considering a pay increase or new hire, the cost-per-employee trend gives you a baseline to work from. Showing that cost per employee has already risen 12% over the past 18 months — even before the proposed increase — contextualises the decision with data.

**Connect headcount growth to revenue forecasts.** If a client is planning to hire significantly, link the headcount plan to the revenue forecast in the Forecasts module. Showing the projected impact on the staff cost ratio under different growth scenarios makes the hiring decision tangible and financially grounded.

**Reference peer benchmarks to validate or challenge staffing decisions.** If a client's revenue per employee is in the bottom quartile but they are planning to hire further, the benchmarking data gives you an evidence-based basis to ask whether the existing team is fully utilised before adding headcount.

<Tip>
  Export the HR report as a PDF to include in your pre-meeting pack. A one-page summary showing headcount, staff cost trend, and peer benchmark position is a concise and visually effective addition to any client report.
</Tip>
