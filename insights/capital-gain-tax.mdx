Capital gains tax planning is one of the highest-value services an accounting firm can offer, yet it is also one of the easiest to miss until it is too late. aiGust's capital gain tax module monitors your clients' balance sheet data continuously, identifying movements that may indicate a disposal or acquisition event, and presenting them in a structured format that helps you prepare for CGT conversations well ahead of any filing deadline.

## What the capital gain tax module shows

The CGT module in aiGust provides a structured summary of potential capital gain events detected across a client's balance sheet. For each identified event, the module shows:

- **Asset description** — the nominal account or asset register entry associated with the movement.
- **Estimated proceeds** — the value received or the balance sheet movement that suggests a disposal.
- **Estimated base cost** — the historic cost recorded in the books, where available.
- **Indicative gain or loss** — the difference between proceeds and base cost, presented as an indicative figure before any reliefs or adjustments.
- **Period of detection** — the accounting period in which the movement was identified.

<Note>
  All figures in the CGT module are indicative only. They are derived from balance sheet movements and accounting entries, not from formal CGT calculations. Always review figures with the client and engage a tax specialist before submitting any CGT-related returns.
</Note>

## How aiGust identifies potential capital gain events

aiGust detects potential CGT events by analysing movements in fixed asset accounts, investment accounts, and other capital balance sheet categories across the client's accounting history.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Balance sheet scanning">
    Each time data syncs, aiGust scans for reductions in asset account balances that are not explained by depreciation or amortisation. A reduction that exceeds the expected depreciation profile flags as a potential disposal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Proceeds identification">
    aiGust searches for corresponding entries — typically on the income side or in a disposal proceeds account — that may represent the proceeds received from the asset movement. Where a matching entry is found, it is linked to the disposal event.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Base cost lookup">
    aiGust looks back through the asset's balance sheet history to identify the earliest recorded cost, which is used as the indicative base cost. If the asset was already on the balance sheet when the client connected to aiGust, the opening balance is used and flagged accordingly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Gain or loss calculation">
    The module calculates the indicative gain or loss as proceeds minus base cost. It does not apply allowances, reliefs, or indexation at this stage — those adjustments require specialist input.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  aiGust cannot detect CGT events that bypass the balance sheet entirely — for example, disposals of personally held assets or share transactions not recorded in the company's books. The module is a screening tool, not a comprehensive CGT review. Always discuss the full picture with your client.
</Warning>

## Reviewing assets and disposals

To access the CGT module, open a client administration, navigate to **Insights**, and select **Capital Gain Tax**.

The screen is divided into two panels:

**Assets panel (left).** Lists all active capital assets aiGust has identified on the balance sheet, grouped by asset category. Click any asset to see its full balance sheet history, including opening cost, additions, depreciation, and any disposal movements.

**Disposals panel (right).** Lists all events that aiGust has flagged as potential disposals in the current and prior periods. Each entry shows the asset name, period, indicative proceeds, indicative base cost, and indicative gain or loss.

<Accordion title="Marking an event as reviewed">
  Once you have assessed a flagged disposal and confirmed its CGT treatment, click **Mark as reviewed** on the disposal card. This moves the event to the **Reviewed** archive and removes it from the active disposals list. Reviewed events remain accessible for audit purposes.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Adding notes to a disposal event">
  Click the **Notes** icon on any disposal card to add a free-text note. Use this to record your initial assessment, any information gathered from the client, or a reminder for follow-up. Notes are visible to all team members with access to the administration.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Flagging an event for specialist review">
  If a disposal is complex — for example, it involves a partial disposal, a related-party transaction, or a significant gain — click **Flag for specialist review**. This adds a tag to the event and generates an item in the **Actions** queue for the assigned team member or specialist.
</Accordion>

## Using the CGT summary in client advisory conversations

The CGT module is designed to support advisory conversations, not replace specialist tax advice. Use it to:

**Open the CGT conversation early.** If aiGust flags a significant disposal in the current period, reach out to the client before the year-end rather than waiting until the tax return is prepared. Early identification of a large gain gives the client time to explore available reliefs, consider the timing of other disposals to offset losses, or structure future transactions more efficiently.

**Present the indicative gain clearly.** Use the CGT summary screen as a visual aid in client meetings. Walk through the asset, the estimated proceeds, and the indicative gain, and explain the next steps required to arrive at a formal CGT figure. Clients appreciate having the numbers in front of them, even when they are provisional.

**Identify patterns across periods.** Reviewing the full disposal history in the CGT module may reveal a pattern of unrecognised disposals or underreported proceeds — issues that could create compliance risk if not addressed. Surfacing these proactively demonstrates the value of continuous monitoring.

**Connect CGT to broader planning.** A disposal often has implications beyond CGT — for example, the proceeds may need to be reinvested, retained earnings distributions may be affected, or the disposal may have VAT implications. Use the CGT conversation as an entry point for a wider advisory discussion.

## Limitations and when to engage a tax specialist

aiGust's CGT module is a detection and screening tool built to help you identify potential CGT events faster. It has the following known limitations:

| Limitation | Explanation |
|---|---|
| No relief calculations | The module does not calculate Business Asset Disposal Relief, Rollover Relief, Gift Relief, or any other CGT reliefs. |
| No indexation | Indexation allowance (for assets held before April 1998) is not applied to base cost figures. |
| Incomplete asset histories | For assets already on the balance sheet at the time of connection, only the opening balance is available as base cost. Pre-connection acquisition history is not visible to aiGust. |
| Personal assets excluded | Assets held personally by shareholders or directors are outside the scope of the module unless they appear in the company's books. |
| Cryptocurrency and digital assets | Non-fungible or unusual asset classes may not be detected reliably by balance sheet movement analysis. |

<Warning>
  Always engage a qualified tax specialist before advising a client on CGT liability, filing a CGT return, or making recommendations about reliefs. The figures produced by aiGust are intended to facilitate your initial review and client conversations — they are not a substitute for a formal CGT computation.
</Warning>
