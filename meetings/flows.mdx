A meeting flow is the backbone of every aiGust meeting. It defines the agenda sections your team works through, the financial data blocks that appear in each section, and the discussion prompts that guide the conversation. Think of a flow as a reusable template — you build it once, and every meeting that references it inherits the same structure automatically.

## What a Meeting Flow Contains

A flow is made up of one or more **sections**. Each section can hold any combination of:

- **Rich text** — headings, bullet points, and prose to frame the discussion.
- **Data blocks** — live financial widgets such as a P&L snippet, KPI card, or chart that pull figures from your connected data source.
- **Discussion prompts** — pre-written questions or talking points that appear as cues during the meeting.
- **Action item placeholders** — slots that remind the presenter to capture a specific type of action item before moving on.

<Note>
  Data blocks inside a flow are configured with a data *type* (for example, "Net Profit — current period vs prior period"), not a hard-coded value. The live figure is resolved when you open a real meeting for a specific client.
</Note>

## Create a Custom Flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Flow Builder">
    Navigate to **Meetings → Flows** in the sidebar and click **New Flow**. Give your flow a name and an optional description that helps colleagues understand when to use it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Your First Section">
    Click **Add Section** and enter a section title — for example, *Revenue Performance* or *Outstanding Receivables*. Each section becomes a heading in the meeting agenda.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Content to the Section">
    Inside the section, use the **+** block menu to insert rich text, data blocks, discussion prompts, or action item placeholders. Arrange blocks by dragging them into the order that suits the conversation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat for Each Agenda Item">
    Add as many sections as your meeting requires. Most quarterly reviews work well with four to six sections to keep the conversation focused within a 45-minute window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the Flow">
    Click **Save Flow**. The flow is saved to your personal workspace and is immediately available when you create a new meeting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep section names short and action-oriented — for example, *Review Cash Position* rather than *Cash*. Clear section titles help both the presenter and the client understand the purpose of each agenda item at a glance.
</Tip>

## Add Sections and Data Blocks

Once you are inside the flow editor, you can enrich any section with data blocks that surface live figures during the meeting.

**To insert a data block:**

1. Place your cursor inside a section and click the **+** icon in the left margin.
2. Select **Data Block** from the menu.
3. Choose a block type from the panel on the right:

| Block Type | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| **P&L Snippet** | Revenue, expenses, and net profit for a selected period |
| **KPI Card** | A single metric with period-over-period comparison |
| **Bar Chart** | Monthly or quarterly trend for any financial line item |
| **Cash Flow Summary** | Operating, investing, and financing cash flows |

4. Configure the block's parameters — period, comparison baseline, and any filters — then click **Insert**.

<Note>
  Data blocks preview with sample figures inside the flow editor. They resolve to real client data only when you open a meeting that uses the flow.
</Note>

## Reuse Flows Across Client Meetings

Flows are client-agnostic by design. When you create a new meeting for any client, you select a flow from the dropdown. aiGust applies the flow's structure to that meeting and binds the data blocks to the selected client's financial data.

This means a single *Quarterly Review* flow can power meetings for all of your clients simultaneously. You never need to rebuild the agenda for each client — only the data changes.

<Tip>
  If a specific client requires a consistently different agenda — for example, a client whose business has an unusual reporting cadence — create a dedicated flow named after that client or engagement type. This keeps your flow library organised without cluttering the default options.
</Tip>

## Share or Publish a Flow to the Whole Firm

By default, a flow you create is visible only to you. To make it available to colleagues, you can share it or publish it firm-wide.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Flow Settings">
    Go to **Meetings → Flows**, find your flow in the list, and click the **⋯** menu. Select **Share Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a Sharing Level">
    Select one of the following options:

    - **Private** — only you can see and use this flow.
    - **Shared with specific people** — enter colleagues' names or email addresses. They can use the flow in their own meetings but cannot edit it unless you also grant edit access.
    - **Published to firm** — every member of your firm can see and use this flow. Only users with the **Flow Editor** or **Admin** role can modify it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm and Save">
    Click **Save Sharing Settings**. Colleagues with access will see the flow appear in their flow dropdown immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Publishing a flow to the firm is a great way to enforce a consistent client experience across your team. Consider publishing your core quarterly and annual review flows and keeping experimental or client-specific flows private or shared with a small group.
</Note>
