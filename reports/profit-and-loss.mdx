The Profit & Loss report is usually the first place accountants look when they open a client file in aiGust. It answers the most immediate question a business owner asks — did we make money this period? — but it also contains the detail needed to explain *why* the result came out the way it did. Understanding how aiGust structures and presents this report makes your review process faster and your client conversations sharper.

## What the P&L Report Shows

The P&L report summarises all income and expenditure for the selected period. It starts with revenue, deducts the direct costs of generating that revenue, and arrives at gross profit. Operating expenses are then deducted to arrive at operating profit. Any non-operating items — interest, tax, or one-off adjustments — are shown below the operating line to arrive at net profit.

The report covers only the income statement. Balance sheet movements are visible in the Balance Sheet and Cash Flow reports. If you want to reconcile the two, the net profit figure at the bottom of the P&L feeds directly into the retained earnings movement on the balance sheet.

## Revenue, Costs, and Profit Line Structure

aiGust maps accounts from the connected bookkeeping file into a standard P&L hierarchy. The top-level structure looks like this:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Revenue" icon="arrow-trend-up" href="/reports/profit-and-loss">
    All trading income accounts. Broken down by income type if the chart of accounts supports it — for example, product revenue, service revenue, and other income appear as sub-groups under the Revenue total.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cost of Goods Sold" icon="boxes-stacked" href="/reports/profit-and-loss">
    Direct costs tied to revenue generation: materials, direct labour, subcontractors, freight-in. The difference between Revenue and COGS is **Gross Profit**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Operating Expenses" icon="receipt" href="/reports/profit-and-loss">
    Overhead and indirect costs: wages and salaries, rent, utilities, marketing, professional fees, depreciation. The difference between Gross Profit and Operating Expenses is **Operating Profit (EBIT)**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Net Profit" icon="circle-check" href="/reports/profit-and-loss">
    The bottom line after interest income, interest expense, and income tax. This is the figure that flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  The account-to-group mapping is controlled by the chart of accounts configuration in aiGust. If a client's bookkeeping uses non-standard account names or types, some accounts may appear under **Uncategorised**. Resolve these by updating the account mapping in **Settings → Chart of Accounts** for that entity.
</Note>

## Summary vs Detailed View

The P&L opens in summary view by default, showing only the top-level group totals. This is the right starting point when you want a quick read on the overall shape of the business.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Summary View">
    Displays Revenue, COGS, Gross Profit, Operating Expenses, Operating Profit, and Net Profit as single lines. Use this for the first pass of any review — if all the summary lines are moving in the expected direction, you may not need to drill further.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Detailed View">
    Expands every account group to show individual account balances. Switch to this view when a summary line looks unusual and you need to identify which specific accounts are driving the movement. Click **Expand All** in the toolbar, or click the **›** arrow next to a specific group to drill into only that section.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Custom Depth">
    Right-click any group header and choose **Expand to level 2** or **Expand to level 3** to control depth selectively. This is useful when you want detail on, say, wages but want every other section to stay collapsed.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Comparing the Current Period to Prior Periods

Period comparison is where the P&L becomes most useful for advisory work. To add a comparison column, click **Compare** in the report toolbar and select **vs Prior Period** or **vs Prior Year**.

Once the comparison is active, aiGust adds three additional columns:

| Column | What it shows |
|--------|---------------|
| **Prior Period** | The absolute value for the comparison period |
| **Variance $** | The difference between current and prior (current minus prior) |
| **Variance %** | The percentage change, calculated as Variance $ ÷ Prior Period |

Favourable variances (higher revenue, lower costs) are shown in green. Unfavourable variances are shown in red. The colour coding applies only when aiGust can determine the direction — for accounts where a higher balance is neither clearly good nor bad, no colour is applied.

<Tip>
  When reviewing a monthly P&L against the prior month, check the **Variance %** column first and sort by it in descending order (click the column header). Accounts with large percentage movements that you can't immediately explain are the ones worth investigating further.
</Tip>

## Identifying Unusual Movements

aiGust surfaces potential anomalies in the P&L automatically. Look for the following signals:

<Accordion title="Flagged accounts">
  Accounts where the balance has moved by more than a configurable threshold (default: 20% or $5,000, whichever is lower) since the prior period are marked with an amber flag icon. Click the flag to see the movement detail and add a review note.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Accounts with no activity">
  If an account had a balance in the prior period but shows zero in the current period, it appears with a grey dash and a tooltip noting "No activity this period." This can indicate a coding error, a ceased expense, or a one-off prior-period item.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Negative expense accounts">
  Expense accounts with a credit balance (shown as a negative number) are highlighted. This typically means a refund has been coded to the expense account, which is sometimes correct but is worth confirming.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Large one-off items">
  Transactions over a threshold you set in the entity's review settings are tagged as **Notable Items**. A summary of notable items for the period appears in the sidebar when you open the P&L. Click any item to jump to the underlying transaction.
</Accordion>

## Common Questions Accountants Ask from the P&L

The following questions guide a structured P&L review. aiGust provides data to answer all of them directly from the report.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Is revenue growing or declining?">
    Compare the revenue total to the prior period and to the same period last year. If revenue is declining, expand the revenue group to identify which income streams are contracting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Is gross margin holding?">
    Divide Gross Profit by Revenue and compare this ratio to prior periods. A declining gross margin often signals pricing pressure, increased input costs, or a mix shift toward lower-margin products or services. Switch the display mode to **Percentages** to read gross margin directly without manual calculation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Which operating expenses are growing fastest?">
    Sort the operating expenses section by **Variance %** to find the fastest-growing cost lines. Cross-reference with the client — is the growth intentional (new hires, expanded premises) or unexpected?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Is the net profit consistent with cash movements?">
    If net profit is strong but cash has declined, the explanation usually lives in working capital (receivables, inventory, payables) or capital expenditure. Refer to the Cash Flow report for the reconciliation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Are there any non-recurring items distorting the result?">
    Check for large items in other income or other expenses. These often represent asset sales, insurance proceeds, or restructuring costs. Understanding whether the underlying trading result is stronger or weaker than the reported net profit matters for advisory.
  </Step>
</Steps>
