The aiGust dashboard is the first screen you see when you open a client entity. It's designed to give you a rapid financial health check in under a minute — without opening a single report. KPIs, trend sparklines, and active alerts are surfaced in one place so you can walk into a client meeting already oriented, or triage a client file quickly during a busy period review.

## What the Dashboard Shows

The dashboard is made up of four main areas:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="KPI Cards" icon="gauge-high">
    Headline financial figures for the current period: Revenue, Gross Profit, Net Profit, Cash Balance, Accounts Receivable, and Accounts Payable. Each card shows the current value alongside a comparison to the prior period, with an arrow indicating direction.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Trend Charts" icon="chart-line">
    Sparkline charts beneath each KPI card showing the last 12 months of data for that metric. Hover over any point to see the exact value for that month. The charts update automatically as new data syncs from the bookkeeping file.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Alerts Panel" icon="bell">
    A sidebar listing active alerts triggered by aiGust's automated analysis engine. Alerts cover anomalies, overdue receivables, unusual transactions, and data quality issues. Each alert links directly to the relevant report or transaction.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Period Selector" icon="calendar">
    The global period control for the dashboard. Changing it updates all KPI cards and chart highlights simultaneously. Defaults to the most recently completed full month.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Customising the Dashboard Layout

The dashboard layout can be adjusted to surface the metrics that matter most for each client.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter edit mode">
    Click the **Customise** button in the top-right corner of the dashboard. The layout switches to edit mode: cards display drag handles and a remove icon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reorder KPI cards">
    Drag any KPI card to a new position in the grid. aiGust supports a 2-column or 3-column grid — toggle between them using the **Layout** toggle in edit mode.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add or remove metrics">
    Click **+ Add Metric** to browse available KPIs. In addition to the default financial KPIs, you can add ratio-based cards (Gross Margin %, Current Ratio, Debtor Days) and custom metrics if any have been configured for the entity.

    To remove a card, click the **×** icon in its top-right corner. Removed cards are not deleted — they can be re-added from the **Add Metric** panel at any time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the layout">
    Click **Save Layout**. The layout is saved per entity and per user — your customisation doesn't affect what a colleague or client sees when they open the same entity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Create a dashboard layout for each type of client in your portfolio — for example, a layout for retail clients that surfaces inventory turnover and margin, and a separate layout for professional services clients that emphasises utilisation and debtor days. Switching between saved layouts takes one click.
</Tip>

## Reading Trend Charts and Period-Over-Period Changes

Each trend chart covers the last 13 months of data by default. The current period is highlighted with a filled bar or point; prior periods appear in a lighter colour.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Monthly view">
    Plots one data point per calendar month. This is the default and works well for most clients. Seasonal patterns — a retail business's Christmas spike, a tax agent's April surge — are immediately visible.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Quarterly view">
    Aggregates data into quarters. Use this for clients with high month-to-month volatility where monthly charts obscure the underlying direction.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Rolling 12-month view">
    Each data point represents the trailing 12-month total. This smooths seasonality entirely and shows whether the business is genuinely growing or declining over a full business cycle. Use it when a client disputes whether a good month represents a real trend or just a seasonal lift.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

To read the comparison arrow on a KPI card:

- **Green ↑** — the metric has improved compared to the prior period (revenue up, expenses down)
- **Red ↓** — the metric has declined (revenue down, expenses up, cash reduced)
- **Grey →** — the metric is within 2% of the prior period; no significant movement detected

Click any KPI card to open the underlying report filtered to the same period. This makes the dashboard a fast navigation layer over the full report suite.

## Alerts and Triggers Surfaced on the Dashboard

The alerts panel on the right side of the dashboard is generated by aiGust's analysis engine. Alerts are categorised by type and severity:

<Accordion title="Data quality alerts (blue)">
  These indicate issues with the underlying bookkeeping data that may affect the accuracy of reports. Examples:
  - Uncategorised transactions above a threshold
  - Bank accounts that haven't synced in the last 48 hours
  - Balance sheet that doesn't balance due to an unmapped account

  Resolve data quality alerts before presenting reports to a client.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Anomaly alerts (amber)">
  Generated when aiGust detects a movement outside the normal range for an account. Examples:
  - An expense account that has doubled compared to its 6-month average
  - Revenue that is more than 30% below the same month last year
  - A receivable balance that has grown for three consecutive months

  Anomaly alerts are starting points for investigation, not conclusions. Click each one to see the accounts and transactions behind the flag.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Overdue receivable alerts (red)">
  Triggered when debtor balances are ageing beyond the thresholds configured in **Settings → Alert Rules**. By default, any receivable over 60 days generates a red alert. These are useful prompts for a conversation with the client about their credit control processes.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Cash flow alerts (red)">
  Triggered when the current cash balance falls below a configurable minimum, or when the projected cash runway (based on the trailing 3-month average burn rate) drops below 60 days. These alerts are designed to prompt early conversations about liquidity before a client reaches a crisis point.
</Accordion>

<Note>
  Alert thresholds are configurable per entity. Go to **Settings → Alert Rules** for the entity to adjust the sensitivity. If a particular client's business is inherently volatile, raising the anomaly thresholds prevents alert fatigue without missing genuinely significant movements.
</Note>

## Using the Dashboard in Client Meetings

The dashboard is designed to be screen-shared or projected during a client meeting. Its visual density is intentionally lower than a detailed report — it's built for conversation, not silent reading.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the client entity before the meeting">
    Load the dashboard two to three minutes before the meeting starts so you've already read the KPIs and alerts. You want to walk in with the story, not discover it live.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start with the headline KPIs">
    Share your screen and begin with the four headline numbers: revenue, gross profit, net profit, and cash. Ask the client whether the numbers match their expectation before you interpret them. Their reaction often tells you what they want to talk about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use trend charts to establish direction">
    Scroll to the trend charts to show whether the current period is an outlier or part of a pattern. A single strong month on a downward trend reads very differently from a single strong month on an upward one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Work through active alerts together">
    Share the alerts panel and walk through any amber or red alerts. Frame them as observations rather than problems: "aiGust has flagged that your receivables have been growing for three months — can you tell me about the customer mix right now?" This positions you as analytical and prepared.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click through to reports as needed">
    When the conversation requires more detail, click the relevant KPI card or alert link to open the underlying report. The transition from dashboard to report is instant and keeps the meeting flowing.
  </Step>
</Steps>
