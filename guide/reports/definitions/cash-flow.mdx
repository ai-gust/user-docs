The cash flow statement explains how an administration's cash changed during the financial year. It shows why a profit doesn't necessarily mean that cash grew by the same amount.

The standard cash flow is the same for every administration. See [Standard definitions](/guide/reports/overview#standard-definitions).

## Structure

The cash flow is derived with the indirect method: from the profit and loss and the changes in the balance sheet. It starts from the cash flow from operating activities, corrects it for the change in working capital, and then adds the cash flows from investments and financing.

Each line takes the accounts starting with the numbers shown. Lines marked with = are calculated from the lines above them. The first line, turnover, is only there as the reference value for percentages, and isn't shown in the chart.
