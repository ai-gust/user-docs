---

The profit and loss and the cash flow both have a **Graphical view** tab. It shows the report as a waterfall: step by step, how you get from the first line to the last, and where the biggest effects are.

## Read the profit and loss waterfall

<Frame caption="The profit and loss of 2025 as a waterfall.">
  <img src="/images/reports/profit-and-loss-waterfall.jpg" alt="Graphical view of the profit and loss for 2025, with a row per line from turnover to result processing; each row shows the change badge, the amount, a bar in the waterfall, the percentage of turnover and the change in percentage points" />
</Frame>

Each row shows one line of the [profit and loss](/guide/reports/definitions/profit-and-loss):

- **The change** compared with the previous year, as a badge
- **The amount**
- **A bar in the waterfall.** Income is green and goes to the right, costs are red and go to the left. Subtotals such as gross margin and EBITDA are shown in your firm's brand colour.
- **%:** the line as a percentage of turnover
- **Δ %:** the change in that percentage compared with the previous year, in percentage points

Each bar starts where the previous one ended, so you see at a glance which costs weigh most on the result.

## Read the cash flow waterfall

The cash flow waterfall shows how the cash at the start of the period turned into the cash at the end. A sentence at the top sums it up, for example: *On 31/12/2025, Bouwwerken Michel had €264,376.41 on their bank account. On 31/05/2026, this was €70,055.11. This is a decrease of €194,321.30.*

<Frame caption="The cash flow of the open year as a waterfall.">
  <img src="/images/reports/cash-flow-waterfall.jpg" alt="Graphical view of the cash flow from cash at 31/12/2025 to cash at 31/05/2026, with the cash flow from operating activities, the change in working capital needed, cash out to investments and cash in through financing in between" />
</Frame>

1. **Cash at the start** of the period
2. **Cash flow from operating activities**
3. **Change in working capital needed**
4. **Cash out to investments**
5. **Cash in through financing**
6. **Cash at the end** of the period

It shows why a profit doesn't necessarily mean that cash grew by the same amount, for example because customers paid later. See [Cash flow definition](/guide/reports/definitions/cash-flow).

## Drill down

Click a line with an arrow to see what it's made of. The chart then shows the lines below it, with a path at the top, for example *Start › Indirect costs › Services*. Click **Back**, or a step in the path, to go back up.

<Frame caption="The change in working capital needed, split into receivables, payables and accrued accounts.">
  <img src="/images/reports/cash-flow-waterfall-drilldown.jpg" alt="Cash flow waterfall drilled down into change in working capital needed, showing receivables, payables and accrued accounts, with a Back button and the path Start, Change in working capital" />
</Frame>

At account level, the chart shows the largest accounts. Click **More** to show more accounts, or **All** to show them all.

<Frame caption="The services, drilled down to account level.">
  <img src="/images/reports/profit-and-loss-waterfall-accounts.jpg" alt="Profit and loss waterfall drilled down to Indirect costs, Services, showing accounts such as 610200 Rent machinery and equipment and 612040 Fuel company cars, with More and All buttons for the remaining accounts" />
</Frame>

Individual figures have the same [cell details](/guide/reports/table/cell-details) as in the table: transactions, contributors, evolution and [comments](/guide/reports/comments).

## Switch years and change the display

Switch years with the arrows at the top right. The waterfall always shows one year at a time.

**Display settings**, next to the year, has these options:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Year-to-date view** | Shows the year so far, compared with the same stretch of time in other years, instead of the full financial year. See [Years, quarters and periods](/guide/reports/table/periods). |
| **Show open periods** | Includes the open financial year, so you can show the current year. See [Years, quarters and periods](/guide/reports/table/periods). |
| **Diff view** | Shows every bar from zero, instead of from where the previous one ended. See [Diff view](#diff-view). |
| **Group by** | Chooses what you see when you drill down to the lowest level, for example **GL account**. See [Detail level](/guide/reports/table/financial-table#detail-level). |

### Diff view

In a normal waterfall, each bar starts where the previous one ended. With **Diff view**, every bar starts at zero. What adds goes to the right, what subtracts goes to the left. That makes it easier to compare the size of the additions and subtractions with each other.
