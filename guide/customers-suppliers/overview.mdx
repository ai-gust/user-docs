---
title: "Customers and suppliers"
description: "See the largest customers and suppliers of an administration, who caused the change compared with last year, and the customer and supplier credit against the peer group."
---

Which customers bring in most of the turnover? Which suppliers drive the costs up? And does the administration get paid faster or slower than its peers? Click **Analysis** in the administration menu, and choose **Customers** or **Suppliers**.

Both pages have the same two parts:

- **Top customers** or **Top suppliers**, with the most important risers and fallers;
- **Customer credit** or **Supplier credit**, with the benchmark of the peer group.

Your clients see the same pages on the **Customers** and **Suppliers** tabs of the [client portal](/guide/client-portal/client-view), if your firm turned them on.

The top customers and suppliers need transactions with business partners, so they only work for administrations from a bookkeeping connector. For an administration from a compliance file, you see the warning **Compliance only** instead. See [Bookkeeping and compliance connectors](/guide/dashboard/data-sync#bookkeeping-and-compliance-connectors).

aiGust adds up the transactions per business partner:

- **Customers:** the turnover, on accounts 70 and 71.
- **Suppliers:** the purchases and services, on accounts 60 and 61, without 618.

In the middle of the ring, you see the financial year, its total, the change compared with the previous year, and the previous year's total below it. Move to another year with the arrows at the top right.

The table next to it shows:

- **Total:** all customers or suppliers together;
- **Subtotal:** the top five together;
- the top five, each with their amount.

The ring shows how much of the total the top five account for. A large share for one or two business partners means the administration depends on them.

### More or fewer business partners

Click **Show more** to add business partners, for example to see the top 8 or top 10. Click **Show less** to remove them again. The **Subtotal** follows.

### Leave a business partner out

Click the eye next to a business partner to leave them out, for example a group company or the **Unknown business partner**: bookings without a business partner. The totals and the risers and fallers are then calculated without them.

The filter icon at the top right lists who you left out. Click the cross next to one to bring them back. Leaving someone out is only temporary, and only for you. When you leave the page and come back, everyone is back.

### See the transactions

Click an amount, in the chart or in a table, to see the transactions behind it. For example, click a customer to see the invoices that make up their turnover. The **Transaction overview** opens, with the date, period, account, business partner, description and amount of each booking.

- **Sort** by date, description or amount.
- **Export** the list with **Export Excel**.
- **Open the attached document,** such as the invoice, with the paperclip icon.

### Most important risers and fallers

Below the ring, two tables show who caused the change compared with the previous year:

- **Most important risers:** the business partners whose amount went up the most;
- **Most important fallers:** the business partners whose amount went down the most.

Both rank by the change in euro, not in percent. That shows who really made the difference. A large customer that buys 10% less can cost more turnover than ten small customers that double. A small supplier that goes from € 100 to € 200 grows by 100%, but hardly matters.

For customers, an increase is good news and shows in green. For suppliers, it's a cost increase and shows in red.

### The same view elsewhere

The same view is available:

- in the financial reports: click a figure and open the **Contributors** tab. See [Contributors](/guide/reports/table/cell-details#contributors);
- on a meeting slide, with the [Top contributors & trends](/guide/meetings/widgets/top-contributors) widget.

There, you choose the accounts yourself, and you can also group by account or by label instead of by business partner. On the **Customers** and **Suppliers** pages, the accounts are fixed and the grouping is always per business partner.

## Customer credit and supplier credit

- **Customer credit:** how many days, on average, customers take to pay.
- **Supplier credit:** how many days, on average, the administration takes to pay its suppliers.

For the formulas, see [Ratios](/guide/reports/ratios#working-capital).

The chart shows the administration's credit per financial year as bars, and the peer group as a band. The table below it shows the values, the quartiles of the peer group and the sample size. See [Benchmarks in charts](/guide/benchmarking/reading-benchmarks#benchmarks-in-charts).

Click **Help** for an explanation in aiGust itself, and use the [KPI type selector](/guide/concepts/kpis#kpi-types) to see the values or their growth.

Customer credit can be negative, when prepayments received and VAT payable are larger than the receivables.
