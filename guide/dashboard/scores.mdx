Two widgets sum up how an administration performed in its last closed financial year, in a single score:

| Widget | Compares the administration with |
|---|---|
| **Performance vs benchmark** | Its [default peer group](/guide/benchmarking/peer-groups#the-default-peer-group) |
| **Administration score** | Fixed thresholds, without a peer group |

Both widgets are on the [administration dashboard](/guide/dashboard/dashboard), and you can also add them to meeting slides.

## How the score works

Both scores work the same way:

1. The administration starts with a score of 5 out of 10.
2. aiGust looks at five KPIs: revenue growth, EBITDA margin, cash flow, liquidity and solvability.
3. For each KPI, the score goes up by 1 if the result is good, stays the same if it's normal, and goes down by 1 if it's weak.

The score, from 0 to 10, is shown as one of five levels:

| Score | Level |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | **Excellent** |
| 7–8 | **Good** |
| 4–6 | **Normal** |
| 2–3 | **Lagging** |
| 0–1 | **Bad** |

An administration that's normal on every KPI keeps its starting score of 5, and is **Normal**. It needs at least two KPIs more on the good side than on the weak side to reach **Good**.

Click the information icon on the widget to see how the score was built up.

## Performance vs benchmark

**Performance vs benchmark** compares the administration's KPIs with its default peer group. For each KPI, it looks at where the administration sits compared with the [benchmark band](/guide/benchmarking/reading-benchmarks) from Q25 to Q75:

| Position | Score |
|---|---|
| Better than the band: among the top performers | +1 |
| Inside the band | 0 |
| Worse than the band: among the underperformers | −1 |

"Better" depends on the KPI: higher revenue growth is better, but for a cost, higher is worse.

| KPI | Question |
|---|---|
| **Revenue growth** | Did the company's revenue grow faster or slower than its peers'? |
| **EBITDA margin** | Is its EBITDA in line with the benchmark? |
| **Cash flow** | Is its cash flow stronger, weaker or in line with that of similar companies? |
| **Liquidity** | How does its ability to meet short-term obligations compare with its peers'? |
| **Solvability** | Is its balance between equity and debt healthier or riskier than its peers'? |

## Administration score

The **Administration score** looks at the administration on its own. Instead of comparing with peers, it checks each KPI against fixed thresholds:

| KPI | Question | −1 | 0 | +1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue growth** | Did revenue increase or decline? | Below −1% | −1% to +2% | Above +2% |
| **EBITDA margin evolution** | Did the company keep a stable EBITDA margin relative to revenue? | Below −1 pp | −1 pp to +1 pp | Above +1 pp |
| **Cash flow** | Did the business generate a positive cash flow? | Negative | Zero | Positive |
| **Liquidity** (current ratio) | Can the company repay its short-term debts? | Below 0.75 | 0.75 to 1.25 | Above 1.25 |
| **Solvability** | Does the company have a healthy balance between equity and debt? | Below 20% | 20% to 35% | Above 35% |

Revenue growth needs to be above 2% to count as good: roughly, growth that beats inflation. EBITDA margin evolution is the [vertical delta](/guide/concepts/kpis#vertical-delta) of EBITDA, in percentage points (pp).
