With **Notifications** on in a financial report, aiGust colours the changes that matter, from red to green. See [Notifications](/guide/reports/table/financial-table#notifications). You decide for your whole firm how sensitive those colours are.

To open the settings, click **Workspace** at the bottom left of the screen, and open the **KPI sensitivity** tab. Your changes are saved right away: there's no **Save** button. On this screen, aiGust calls a coloured figure an *alert*. These alerts are separate from the alerts of [triggers](/guide/triggers/how-triggers-work).

## How aiGust decides on the colour

aiGust looks at two things for each figure:

1. **How important the KPI is:** how large it is compared with its reference value. For a cost, that's usually the turnover. A cost of 0.05% of turnover doesn't matter much. A cost of 5% does.
2. **How large the change is:** compared with the previous period, or with the peer group.

aiGust multiplies the two. A big change in a small cost gets no colour, or a light one. The same change in your largest cost gets a strong one.

## Which KPIs matter

Under **Which KPIs Matter?**, you set from what size a KPI counts, as a percentage of its reference value:

| Level | Default | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Below **Interesting** | | No colour |
| **Interesting** | From 1% | 1 |
| **Important** | From 5% | 2 |
| **Very Important** | From 10% | 3 |

Drag the points on the slider, or type the percentages in the fields.

### Exceptions

Some KPIs always matter, whatever their size. Others never do.

- **Always Trigger:** these KPIs always count as very important. By default, that's EBITDA, EBIT, net result and result to assign, and the four cash flow totals.
- **Never Trigger:** these KPIs never get a colour. By default, the list is empty.

Click **Add Exception** to add a KPI to a list. Click the cross next to a KPI to remove it.

## Deviation from previous period

Under **Deviation from Previous Period**, you set how large a change compared with the previous period has to be:

| Level | The change is | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **Very Good** | Above the **Very Good** percentage | +2 |
| **Good** | Above the **Good** percentage | +1 |
| | Between **Fair** and **Good** | No colour |
| **Fair** | Below the **Fair** percentage | −1 |
| **Bad** | Below the **Bad** percentage | −2 |
| **Very Bad** | Below the **Very Bad** percentage | −3 |

For a KPI where an increase is bad news, such as a cost, aiGust turns the sign around. A cost that rises by 20% counts as a drop of 20%.

## Deviation from benchmark

Under **Deviation from Benchmark**, there's nothing to set. aiGust checks whether the figure falls between the 25th and the 75th percentile of the [peer group](/guide/benchmarking/how-it-works):

- **Between the two:** no colour. The administration performs around average.
- **Outside:** a colour, red on the bad side and green on the good side. How strong it is depends only on how important the KPI is.

You see this colour on the **Benchmark** tab of the [cell details](/guide/reports/table/cell-details).

## The result

The importance score times the deviation score gives the colour:

| Importance × deviation | Level |
|---|---|
| +3 or more | Great |
| +1 or +2 | Good |
| −1 or −2 | Minor |
| −3 to −8 | Major |
| −9 | Critical: a very important KPI with a very bad change |

For example: an important KPI (2) with a bad change (−2) gives −4, a major alert. An interesting KPI (1) with the same change gives −2, a minor one.

## For one administration

The settings apply to every administration of your firm. You can't change them for one administration. You can only switch off the colours of one line: open the cell details of a figure, and turn off **Alerts** at the top of the panel. See [Notifications in the cell details](/guide/reports/table/cell-details#notifications).
