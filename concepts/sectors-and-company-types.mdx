Every client administration in aiGust carries two classification attributes: a **sector** and a **company type**. These two settings feed directly into the benchmarking engine, telling aiGust which peer group to measure your client against. Setting them correctly from the start means every KPI comparison, alert threshold, and industry insight your client sees is grounded in relevant data — not a generic average.

## What Sectors Are

A sector is the industry or market segment in which a client's business operates. aiGust ships with a curated list of sectors drawn from standard economic classification frameworks, grouped into broad categories for ease of selection.

Examples include:

- Retail & E-commerce
- Construction & Real Estate
- Hospitality & Food Service
- Professional Services
- Manufacturing
- Healthcare & Social Care
- Transport & Logistics

When you assign a sector, aiGust uses it to pull the correct peer benchmarks for metrics like gross margin, overhead ratio, and debtor days. A hospitality business is benchmarked against other hospitality businesses — not against professional service firms with structurally different cost bases.

<Note>
  Benchmarking data is refreshed on a rolling basis as aggregated, anonymised data from administrations in the same sector is updated. The more precisely you define the sector, the tighter and more meaningful the peer group becomes.
</Note>

## What Company Types Are

A company type describes the legal and structural form of the client's business. aiGust supports the most common entity structures encountered in an accounting practice.

## Why Correct Classification Matters

Assigning the wrong sector or company type does not break aiGust, but it degrades the quality of every insight that depends on peer data.

- **Benchmarking accuracy** — Gross margin norms for a construction SME differ significantly from those for a retail sole trader. Misclassification means your client is measured against an irrelevant cohort.
- **Alert calibration** — Alert thresholds for metrics like debtor days and overhead ratio are sector-adjusted. A threshold that is healthy for one sector may signal a problem in another.
- **Report language** — Some report templates use sector-specific terminology and commentary. The correct sector ensures those labels and narrative prompts are contextually appropriate.
- **Meeting preparation** — aiGust's meeting tools surface sector-specific talking points. Accurate classification makes those prompts more relevant and reduces preparation time.

## How to Set or Change Sector and Company Type

You can set or update these attributes at any point from within the administration settings.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the administration">
    From the firm portfolio, click the client's name to enter their administration context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to Administration Settings">
    In the left-hand sidebar, click **Settings**, then select **Administration Details**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the classification fields">
    Locate the **Sector** and **Company Type** fields. Click either field to open a searchable dropdown. Type to filter the list, then click your selection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your changes">
    Click **Save Changes** at the bottom of the form. aiGust recalculates all benchmarks and sector-dependent insights immediately. Depending on the volume of historical data, the refresh may take up to a few minutes to propagate across all reports.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If you are onboarding a batch of new clients, you can set sector and company type in bulk from the firm-level portfolio view. Select multiple administrations using the checkboxes, then choose **Edit Classification** from the bulk actions menu.
</Tip>
