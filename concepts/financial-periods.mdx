Financial periods are the time boundaries that tell aiGust which data to include in any given report, KPI calculation, or meeting preparation view. Getting the period configuration right — particularly the financial year start date — ensures that every figure aiGust surfaces aligns with the way your client's business actually measures performance, rather than defaulting to a generic calendar year.

## What a Financial Period Is

In aiGust, a financial period is any defined span of time used to scope financial data. Periods can take several forms:

- **Fiscal year** — The twelve-month period that constitutes the client's official financial year. This is the primary period type used for annual reporting and year-on-year comparisons.
- **Calendar month** — A standard month (January, February, etc.), used for monthly management accounts and short-term trend analysis.
- **Quarter** — A three-month block aligned to either the fiscal year or the calendar year, depending on the administration's settings.
- **Custom range** — Any start and end date you define manually, useful for ad-hoc analysis or mid-year review meetings.
- **Year-to-date** — The period from the start of the current fiscal year to today's date, updated dynamically as each day passes.

## Setting the Financial Year Start

The financial year start date is the most important period setting in an administration. It defines where each annual period begins and ends throughout the platform.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Administration Settings">
    Inside the administration, click **Settings** in the left-hand sidebar, then select **Financial Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Locate the Financial Year Start field">
    Find the **Financial Year Start** field. It displays the current setting as a month and day — for example, *1 April* for a UK-standard tax year.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the start date">
    Click the field and select the month and day on which this client's financial year begins. If the client's year runs from 1 July to 30 June, select **July 1**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and confirm">
    Click **Save Changes**. aiGust will ask you to confirm, as this change re-segments all historical period data. Click **Confirm** to proceed. The recalculation typically completes within a few minutes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Changing the financial year start retroactively re-assigns all historical transactions to new period buckets. Any saved period-specific annotations or meeting notes remain attached to their original data points but may now sit in a differently labelled period.
</Note>

## Switching Between Periods

The period selector is available on every data view in aiGust — dashboards, reports, and the meetings preparation screen.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the period selector">
    Look for the date range control in the top-right area of any data view. It displays the currently active period, for example *FY 2024* or *January 2025*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a period type">
    Click the selector to open the period picker. Choose from **Year**, **Quarter**, **Month**, or **Custom** using the tabs at the top of the picker.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the specific period">
    For year, quarter, and month views, use the navigation arrows to move backward or forward through available periods. For custom ranges, enter a start date and end date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply">
    Click **Apply** (or simply click a period in the year/quarter/month tabs). The page data refreshes immediately to reflect the selected period.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Year-to-Date vs Full-Year Views

These two views answer different questions and are both accessible from the period selector.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Year-to-Date (YTD)" icon="calendar-days" href="/reports/dashboard">
    Shows data from the first day of the current financial year up to today. YTD figures are dynamic — they grow each day and are the most relevant view for live monitoring and client check-ins during the year.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Full Year" icon="calendar" href="/reports/profit-and-loss">
    Shows data for a complete, closed financial year. Use this view for annual review meetings, year-end reporting, and multi-year comparisons. aiGust marks incomplete current-year data clearly so you never confuse a partial year with a closed one.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  When preparing for a mid-year client meeting, use the **YTD** view alongside the **Prior Year (Same Period)** comparison to show how the business is tracking at the same point last year. This combination gives clients an immediately intuitive read on performance trajectory.
</Tip>

## Period Comparisons

aiGust supports structured period comparisons directly within the dashboard and reports.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Current Year vs Prior Year">
    The most common comparison mode. Select **Prior Year** in the comparison dropdown to place the current financial year side by side with the preceding one. Both full-year and YTD views support this comparison. Variances are shown in absolute terms, percentage terms, and visually via trend indicators.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Current Period vs Prior Period">
    For month-by-month monitoring, select **Prior Period** to compare, for example, March against February, or Q3 against Q2. This view highlights short-term momentum and helps identify emerging issues before they affect the annual figures.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Custom Period Comparison">
    Define two arbitrary date ranges to compare. Navigate to any report, click the comparison control, and select **Custom**. Enter a primary range and a comparison range. aiGust renders both datasets in parallel. Use this for one-off analyses such as comparing the six months before and after a pricing change.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Budget vs Actual">
    If a budget file has been imported for the current financial year, choose **Budget** as the comparison target to display actuals alongside planned figures. Variance columns highlight where performance diverges from plan. See the Budgets guide for details on importing a budget.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
